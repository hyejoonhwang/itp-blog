## Intro
- The future shows up first as small, weird experiments at the edges (the **fringe**). When you connect enough of them, you see a **trend**.
- Being successful can make you stop looking. BlackBerry compared _products_ instead of watching the _trend_.
- A futurist doesn't predict the future like a psychic. She gathers evidence, spots trends, and figures out which futures are more or less likely.
## Chapter 1: The drone that reshaped America (in theory)

Webb says you need to be **"chronologically ambidextrous."** That means handling today's work with one hand while thinking about the far future with the other, like a pianist playing different parts with each hand.

- Every decision touches other systems, so zoom out and follow the ripple effects.
- Warning signs that look separate are often one pattern. Sony saw a string of isolated attacks instead of a trend.
- Our brains resist change and get stuck in the present. You need a deliberate process to get around that.
- **Webb's six step method** (the rest of the book):
    1. Find the fringe
    2. Look for patterns (CIPHER)
    3. Ask hard questions to test the trend
    4. Figure out timing
    5. Write future scenarios and a strategy
    6. Pressure test the strategy
